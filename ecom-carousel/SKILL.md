# E-Commerce Carousel

Create a compact, specific Carousel that helps a shop customer understand, compare, decide, use, or remember something.

## Establish the single job

Identify the product, audience, customer question, buying phase, content goal, and verified facts. If important product information is missing, ask briefly or use visible placeholders.

Read [references/carousel-patterns.md](references/carousel-patterns.md) and choose one structure.

## Build the Carousel

1. Write three cover options with different approaches.
2. Choose the strongest option and explain the choice in one sentence.
3. Default to four or five slides.
4. Give every slide one job and a clear transition to the next.
5. Keep text to roughly three short lines or fewer per slide unless a precise comparison needs slightly more.
6. Use concrete product, customer, or shop examples.
7. Make the final slide a useful action, question, save prompt, comparison step, or next buying action.
8. Add a visual note for each slide.

## Default output

Return:

1. **Goal and buying phase**
2. **Three cover options**
3. **Recommended cover**
4. **Final slide copy** in a table with slide, purpose, exact text, and visual direction
5. **Design notes** — hierarchy, product imagery, whitespace, and emphasis
6. **Caption bridge** — what the caption should add rather than repeat
7. **Transparency note** — assumptions or claims needing evidence

## Carousel rules

- Keep four to five slides as the standard. Use more only when the user requests it or the information cannot stay accurate and useful otherwise.
- Do not place labels such as “Hook,” “Problem,” or “CTA” in the visible slide copy.
- Avoid crowded covers, paragraph slides, vague motivation, and listicles with no product relevance.
- Do not repeat the same sentence in the caption.
- Do not invent product benefits, comparisons, reviews, tests, statistics, or customer results.
- Prefer one strong example over several shallow examples.
- Write in natural, direct language that fits mobile reading.

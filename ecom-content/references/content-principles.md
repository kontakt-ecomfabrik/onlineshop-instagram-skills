# Content principles

## Build from commerce reality

Start from this chain:

**Product → problem or wish → use → customer question → objection → outcome**

Choose the relevant parts; do not force every post to use the full chain.

Prefer these evidence sources:

1. Verified product details and demonstrations
2. Real customer questions and support conversations
3. Review patterns supplied or verified by the user
4. Search questions and marketplace language
5. Purchase barriers, comparisons, care, fit, sizing, delivery, use, and aftercare
6. Founder or production insight that is true and relevant

## Write like a shop, not a coach

Prefer:

- “Can I wear this chain in the shower?”
- “Which mask fits dry skin?”
- “Why can two products with the same material feel different?”
- “What happens after you place the order?”

Avoid defaulting to:

- “Three mistakes stopping your success”
- “Nobody talks about this”
- “Stop scrolling”
- “This will change everything”
- artificial controversy or fear

## Voice

- Natural, direct, calm, honest, practical, and specific
- Clear enough to understand on the first read
- Human rhythm; contractions and fragments are acceptable when natural
- Minimal jargon; explain necessary terms plainly
- No inflated promises, empty motivation, or synthetic “AI voice”
- Use customer language without mocking, stereotyping, or manipulating

## Accuracy and transparency

- Distinguish known facts, user statements, reasonable inferences, and open questions.
- Never fabricate evidence or imply personal testing that did not happen.
- Use placeholders such as `[Material]`, `[Preis]`, or `[Beleg]` when a precise claim needs input.
- For regulated or time-sensitive claims, verify current primary sources when research is authorized; otherwise state the limit and keep the wording factual.
- Do not promise virality, sales, ranking, reach, or revenue.

## Inclusion and visuals

- Represent shop owners and customers across genders, ages, skin tones, bodies, and backgrounds when people appear.
- Avoid generic stock-photo concepts when authentic product, hands, process, packaging, or real environment footage is possible.
- Keep visuals realistic to the product color, scale, material, and use.
